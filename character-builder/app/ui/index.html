      Own 3D model

      Give your fighter its own PS1-style 3D model. A model with a skeleton (rigged) is used as it is; a model without a skeleton is rigged by the creator: it stands the model upright, finds the joints and shows them so you can check or move them. The creator fits it to the fighting style's skeleton and replaces the style's model on the costume you choose.

      What makes a model work well?
      • Human shape: a head, two arms and two legs. With a skeleton (Mixamo, Character Creator, Ready Player Me, VRoid and most game rigs) it is used as it is; unusual bone names are recognised from the skeleton's shape. Without a skeleton the creator rigs it: it finds the head, hands and feet, places the joints, and you can drag any joint that is off. Lying, upside-down or back-facing models are turned upright (or turn them yourself with one click).
      • Standing in T-pose or A-pose, arms away from the body.
      • Chibi builds (a big head on short legs) get longer legs: Tekken's animations need the hips at about half the height, so the legs are stretched until they are; the head and arms stay as they are.
      • File: .fbx (FBX Binary), .glb or .vrm (VRoid), up to 16 MB, with the texture embedded. Models with only material colours work too.
      • Any size: Tekken 3 fighters have 650-1100 triangles. A bigger model is reduced to that size and gets a new texture made for it (1 to 3 minutes).
      • Hair, tails and horns can't move on their own in Tekken 3: parts that stick out are folded down for you, and you can change that per part.
      • Clothes become part of the body shape. Long coats, capes and wide skirts move with the legs, so they look best when they are short or tight.

          Skeleton

          Your model came without a skeleton, so the creator made one: it found the head, hands and feet, followed the body to the shoulders and hips and put the joints in human proportions. Each dot should sit inside the body on its joint. Drag a dot to move it (front view: left-right and up-down; side view: front-back), then press Use these joints. Try a pose moves the model on its new skeleton: every part should follow its own bone.

          A piece that moves with the wrong bone (a coat tail on a leg, hair on a shoulder)? Pick the body part it should follow and paint over it in either view. Painting goes through the model (front and back at once).

          🎨 Colours

          Pick a colour, then paint on the model. 🖌 Paint: drag over it. 🪣 Fill: click a piece to colour all of it (a shirt, a boot). 🧍 Body part: click to colour a whole part (head, upper arm, thigh…), handy for a model in one grey piece. 💧 Pick: take a colour from the model. Turn the model: right-drag (or ✋ Turn), zoom with the wheel, Shift-drag to move it.

              Parts that stick out

              Tekken 3 can't swing hair, tails or horns: they stay where they are modelled. They are folded down for you; change it per part if you like.

                Detail settings apply to big models, which are reduced to PS1 size. Less face detail leaves more for the body; less body detail gives a simpler, more PS1-like model.

                  Moves

                  The moves your fighter gets from its fighting style, as Tekken 3's COMMAND LIST shows them. 1 left punch, 2 right punch, 3 left kick, 4 right kick; d, df, f, b, u: directions (capitals: hold); N: neutral; (*): the move other entries refer to. Below: change the damage, or put other moves of this style on the basic inputs.

                  Moves: damage

                  Damage of each basic input of this style, standing, read from the moveset on your disc (first hit of the move; a few styles do something else from a stance or crouch). Change a number to give your fighter other damage: it applies only to your fighter, not to the style's own fighter, and not in a mirror match (both players then share one moveset).

                  Moves on buttons

                  Put another move of this style on a basic input: the input then starts that move (a string's first hit brings the rest of the string along). The last lines, where a style has them: a string's later hit, so the string ends in another move. Moves of this style only, as its animations are the style's own; throws stay on their own inputs. Like the damage: your fighter only, not in a mirror match.

                  Intro and win pose

                  Tekken 3 draws one of the style's intros at the start of a fight and one of its win poses after a won round. Choose one to always see it (your fighter only).

                  Fairness check

                  Your changes against Tekken 3's own cast: green is within what the cast does (its strongest but one on that input), amber up to a quarter above, red beyond. Saved with the fighter and carried in its package.
